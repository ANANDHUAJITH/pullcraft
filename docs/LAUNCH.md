# Getting people to find, star and contribute

Nobody can guarantee stars. These are the things that move the needle for projects like this, in order.

1. **A world that isn't empty.** The repo ships four demo plots (`plots/demo-*`). Keep a few until real builders arrive.
2. **A 20-second GIF at the top of the README** of: fly over the city → open a plot → press Code → preview. Record it once the site is live and put it in `docs/hero.gif`.
3. **A one-click demo.** Turn on Pages and link the live URL in the repo "About" box (`scripts/launch-setup.sh` does this).
4. **Topics.** `scripts/launch-setup.sh` sets: `minecraft`, `voxel`, `threejs`, `github-actions`, `github-pages`, `webgl`, `game`, `procedural-generation`, `css`, `generative-ai`, `open-source`, `hacktoberfest`.
5. **Good first issues.** The script creates a dozen labelled starter issues so newcomers have something to claim in minutes.
6. **Post where builders hang out**, with the GIF: Hacker News (Show HN), r/webdev, r/threejs, r/minecraft, r/proceduralgeneration, Lobsters, dev.to, and X/Bluesky. Lead with "every building is a pull request", not the tech stack.
7. **Make the first PR delightful.** Reply to first-time builders within a day. Feature builds in Discussions → Show and tell.
8. **Run an event.** "Build a landmark in CSS" weekends, AI-prompt challenges, a monthly "plot of the month".
9. **Keep shipping small releases** with notes. Activity is what makes people watch a repo.

## Collaborators

Invite regular contributors as collaborators with *Triage* access first (Settings → Collaborators). Label issues `good first issue` and `help wanted`, review PRs fast, and credit people in release notes.
